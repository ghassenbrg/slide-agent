# Critique 2 — fresh-eyes review of the "One Sentence" release candidate

Watched at full size and downscaled to 480 px wide (frames every 10, plus 3–6-frame steps around each cut). Prioritised by how much each problem makes the film feel like "slides moving from scene to scene".

## 1. Six of seven cuts dip through an almost empty frame (highest impact)
At 203–214, 884–892, 1000–1010, 1118–1128, 1236–1250 and 1382–1394 the outgoing picture fades out while the incoming one fades in. For 4–10 frames the screen is a grey, half-transparent ghost of two layouts. That's the slideshow cross-dissolve the client rejected. Range is the worst because it repeats three times in 9 s: drop, dissolve, rise.
**Fix:** no opacity dips. Outgoing material recedes as a physical object, smaller, darker and pushed back. Incoming material rises over it at full opacity, so each new deck is visibly dealt on top of the last one, in time with the request re-typing.

## 2. The close is a 7-second freeze (1440–1650)
After the URL lands, nothing moves except a 20 %-opacity wall. The invitation appears at 1439, two seconds before it's spoken (1500), and the URL is spoken at 1563–1610 with no visual response. A LinkedIn viewer who's still watching gets a still frame.
**Fix:** tie the close to the narration. "Explore the examples" and "Install guide" each land as their words are spoken. A light sweep crosses the URL pill as the URL is read. The lockup gets a slow push, and the deck wall drifts at a readable pace. The URL stays on screen for over 7 s.

## 3. The deliverable fold starts from a layout nobody saw (1242–1270)
The five Fern slides fly into the .pptx from a two-row contact sheet that was cut from the deck tour in an earlier revision. Right after the cut, a lone tilted thumbnail appears out of nowhere (1246–1250) over an empty stage.
**Fix:** the five slides are dealt into that layout first, rising over the receding data deck, then fold into the file in sequence. The file is sealed as the last one goes in.

## 4. The opening's first two seconds are an empty box (0–62)
The hook frame is a dim dashed rectangle while the request types. The canvas doesn't react to the words, so the link between request and result only starts at Send.
**Fix:** the canvas listens. As "launch plan", "timeline", "budget" and "risks" are typed, the matching slot of the real dashboard draws itself in the canvas. After Send, each keyword flies into its own slot, and the slot is replaced by the real component.

## 5. The opening → install cut drops the hero result (203–214)
The dashboard sinks and fades before the install card exists, a dead beat right after the brand moment.
**Fix:** this is covered by item 1. The dashboard pushes back under the composer while the install card rises over it.

## 6. Deliver → close goes to black before the lockup (1382–1394)
**Fix:** the open PowerPoint slide shrinks back into the deck wall as the wall fades up, so the lockup lands on an already-living background.

## Noted, not changed
- **Workflow result is thumbnail-sized (480–593).** It reads as a session UI, and the very next shot rises out of that strip into full slides. Enlarging it would break the strip hand-off, which is one of the film's best connected moves.
- **The install card is mostly static (250–340).** Its motion is the agent chips lighting as they're named, which is the explanation the scene needs. Five seconds is short, and the brief asks for installation to stay brief.
- **The opening caption "This is Slide Agent." repeats the lockup.** It's the spoken line, and sound-off viewers need it.
- **Small slide text in full views is texture on a phone.** The close-ups carry the detail, so this is deliberate.
- **Honesty labels are small (26–28 px).** They stay legible at 480 px and are disclosures, not messages.
