# Showcase decks

Six briefs that force different visual reasoning, each authored as its own
scene. They exist to answer one question honestly: **does an AI using Slide
Agent produce six designs, or one design six times?**

`similarity-report.json` is the measured answer. Regenerate it with:

```bash
npm run examples:evaluate
```

| Deck | Brief | Visual thesis | Composition grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| `technical-architecture` | Approve one anti-corruption boundary instead of six adapters | **Boundary ledger** — a working ledger, not a deck to watch | A numbered rail down the left edge; full-measure rows separated by hairlines; no panels |
| `cultural-heritage` | Fund one more excavation season before the sea takes the trench | **Trench section** — the deck reads downward the way a trench does | Torn horizontal bands of unequal depth; marginalia in a right gutter |
| `travel` | Book the September departure and understand the tide window | **Tide window** — atmosphere carries the page, logistics sit in a band at the foot | Full-bleed plates with one solid band of type; never text directly on an image |
| `board-decision` | Close the Northfield line and redeploy its volume | **Single number** — one figure per page, nowhere to look away to | Extreme sparsity; at most one figure and one sentence per slide |
| `scientific-explanation` | Understand why a lake turns over | **Phase map** — six pictures sharing one depth axis | A vertical axis at a fixed position on every slide; only the plotted quantity changes |
| `fashion-launch` | Place a first order for a nine-piece collection | **Wall text** — a gallery wall, not a line sheet | Type at architectural scale, allowed to run off the edge; one plate per slide, never captioned |

## What each deck carries

Every scene records its own:

- **design exploration** — at least two theses that differ *structurally*, and
  which one it chose, with why the other was rejected;
- **sequence plan** — one entry per slide: its narrative job, what should
  dominate it, its intended silhouette, and its energy;
- **claim and source ledgers** — what is asserted, what backs it, as of when,
  and whether it is verified;
- **visual system** — its own variables, named styles, motifs, and hard
  constraints, in its own vocabulary.

None of that is rendered. It is what makes a later critique possible: the review
packet puts each slide's declared intent next to its actual render and asks
whether the one produced the other.

## Independence

No two decks share a bbox skeleton, a palette transformation, or a scene. The
similarity report compares every pair by geometry signature — occupancy,
dominant mass, centroid, alignment, whitespace topology, reading path — with
colour weighted at a twentieth, so a recoloured copy of a deck reads as the same
deck rather than as a new one.

The report includes a deliberate control: a palette-only restyle of one showcase
deck, built and measured alongside the real ones. It scores as a near-duplicate,
which is the check that the metric is measuring composition rather than paint.

## Imagery

Three decks use plates generated by `scripts/generate-showcase-assets.ts`. They
are abstract fields — a horizon, a stratigraphic face, a hard diagonal — and
every one is declared `provenance.generated` in its scene. Nothing in them
depicts a real place, product, or person, and no scene captions them as if it
did.

A repository cannot ship licensed photography, and a grey box labelled "image
here" is not a design. Generating the plates and declaring them is the honest
third option.

## Readiness

Five of the six report `presentationReadiness: "ready"`. `board-decision`
reports `review`, on purpose: its closure-cost estimate is marked
`needs-review` in the claim ledger because management has not had it
independently reviewed, and the deck says so on the slide. That is the ledger
doing its job — a number nobody has checked should not let a deck call itself
finished.

## Rebuilding them

```bash
npm run examples:showcase
```

Each deck is built with rendering and the clean-directory round-trip gate on, so
the package that lands in `examples/showcase/output/` is one that provably
rebuilds somewhere else.

## Human evaluation

Structural diversity is measurable. "Does this look premium, and does it look
designed for this subject?" is not, and it is the question that actually
matters. `docs/human-evaluation.md` is the blinded protocol used to answer it,
along with the release thresholds and how the raw results are recorded.
